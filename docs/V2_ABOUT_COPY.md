## "01, before Sproutflow" — two paragraphs

> Enterprise work is strict about the parts nobody brags about. You write the scope down. You agree what done means before anyone builds. You test it, and you document the handover so the thing survives the person who made it. That is not talent, it is habit, and it is the reason enterprise software keeps running long after the original team has moved on.
>
> Small businesses almost never get any of that, and it is not a money problem. It is that most studios skip it. I did not want to run an agency and I did not want to hire my way into one. I wanted to take those habits and shrink them down to something a person running their own shop can actually afford, which is why it is one person start to finish and why you own everything when we are done.

## The Sproutflow origin — recommended as its own block

This is the strongest material in the whole set and it is currently not in the mockup at all. Suggest adding a short section between the opening and "before Sproutflow," or using the first paragraph as a pull quote in accent italic.

> New Orleans runs on people who work for themselves. Contractors, practices, firms, offices, trades, shops. It is a city built on doing your own thing, and word of mouth here is strong enough that plenty of them never needed a website to stay busy. Some of the best ones I found had a backlog they could not clear and no website at all.
>
> That is the part that got me. Not that these businesses were failing, but that they were succeeding on reputation alone, with nothing built to catch what comes next. A city that lives on entertainment and tourism, full of people who are genuinely great at what they do, and most of them are invisible the moment someone searches instead of asks. Sproutflow is me working on that, one business at a time.

## Open flag: Portland

Ben said Portland is where he has felt most himself and that he intends to return, and that he is in New Orleans for now to be near family.

That is real and it is the most human thing he said. **It is left out of every draft above on purpose,** and this is a judgment call he should make, not me.

The problem: a prospective New Orleans client reading "I plan to leave" hears "will he still be here when my site breaks?" Sproutflow's whole premise is that you get one person who stays reachable after launch. Signaling impermanence works directly against it.

**Options:**
1. **Leave it out** (what the drafts do). Safest for the client audience he named.
2. **Keep the roots, drop the plan.** Something like "my mom's side is out in Washington and I spent a few years in Portland" is true, adds texture, and reads as a person with a history rather than someone with one foot out the door.
3. **Say it directly.** Only if he wants the site to reflect that he works nationwide and location is genuinely not the point. It is honest, and some clients will respect it, but it costs local trust and should be a deliberate trade.

Recommend option 2 if it goes in at all.
